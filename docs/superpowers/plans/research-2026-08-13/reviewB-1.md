**PART II (Track B) — product & design coherence review. Ranked, 10 strongest.**

**1. [blocker] The identity mark can't deliver its own stated rationale: 32 tokens, eyes deferred, and the assignment rule is self-contradictory.** §10 (line 246) claims "4 silhouettes × 8 hues = 32, assigned by stable hash of the immutable `name` slug (no config, no migration; collision-shifted within the wheel)". Three problems compound: (a) 32 slots gives ~79% chance of at least one duplicate mark at 10 sessions and guaranteed reuse past 32 — this user runs 20 worktrees/sessions (memory: `concurrent-agents-in-repo`), so collision is the norm, and a duplicate mark in an 18px rail reproduces exactly deep-1 §8's "two sessions in the same directory are visually identical"; (b) "collision-shifted" is incompatible with "stable hash of the immutable name" — a shift computed against the live roster changes a session's mark when *another* session is created/deleted, and §12.6's "reroll affordance" requires a persisted per-session override, contradicting "no config, no migration" in the same bullet; (c) deep-3 §1.4:80-82 says the token is **shape × colour × eye-style** *precisely because* "a pure colour chip would not [survive] colour-blindness", yet risk-table line 338 defers eyes ("geometric silhouettes, not faces-with-eyes v1") — leaving a colour-blind user 4 distinguishable tokens for 20 sessions. Also unresolved: §10 says the mark "replaces the status dot", so at 18px status is encoded by *pose only* — the weakest channel — while §12.1's rows still carry mark + StatusDot + unread dot (three coloured circles at 53px). **Fix:** persist `mark_shape`/`mark_hue` columns (default = hash at create, deterministic dedupe against the live roster once, then frozen; agent/user editable — this also gives §15.1 duplicate-session the Grok "copy carries the avatar" behaviour); ship a third *monochrome-safe* channel in v1 (2–3 pupil/notch variants → 96 tokens) instead of deferring it; and keep a status channel that survives 18px (ring or dot), decided in B0 rather than "over time".

**2. [blocker] Board removal orphans the board's *write* path — agents keep reporting into a store with zero read surfaces, which is the exact phantom-feature pathology §10 condemns.** §12.8 removes page + nav + palette verbs + switcher + team read-through boards while "the server-side board API + hook protocol stay initially". But the writers are live and plural: `server/src/board/dispatch.rs` injects issue title/acceptance/comments into sessions via steering, `server/src/board/claim.rs`, `server/src/scheduler/hook.rs`, and critically `server/src/teams/board_sync.rs` — a **read-through mirror of `~/.claude/tasks/{team}/NN.json` onto per-team boards, reconciled every detect tick**. That mirror is the only drill-down behind the `X/Y tasks` rollup on `team-card.tsx:241` / `team-rollup-badges.tsx:88`, and Track A's guard (`!team`, plan line 20) means transcript system lines can never replace it. §12.8's replacement list ("task state per session → transcript system lines + session detail") never specs the session-detail surface, and nothing replaces Done-lane PR/commit links, acceptance criteria, due dates, `team:`-tag assignees, or `replyOrCommentIssue`'s durable-comment fallback for a **dead** session (deep-1 §3:118-119) — a chat renderer over a live pty structurally cannot hold that. **Fix:** make B2 ship a read surface before removal — a per-session/per-team issue list in the session detail sheet (acceptance items + PR link + comment fallback), or stop the writers in the same PR. "API stays, UI goes" is not a neutral state when four server components write to it continuously.

**3. [major] §13.1 and §14 keep `board issue` as a first-class entity chip and picker result after §12.8 deletes the only route that can render one.** §13.1 lists "board issue linked/done" chips and §14's typed union is "session · file · **board issue** · schedule · snippet/skill · host · action" — every one of those chips/results is a dead navigation target post-B2. **Fix:** either define the issue surface (shell-scoped overlay per §11.4, reusing the existing `BoardDetailPane`) or delete `board issue` from both unions and say so.

**4. [major] The attention model adds a *fourth and fifth* vocabulary rather than aligning the existing ones — and drops `error` and `starting` entirely.** In play after Track B: mark `data-state` = 5 values (`idle|thinking|working|waiting|stopped`, §10:247); `SessionStatus` = 6 (`starting|active|waiting|idle|stopped|error`, `web/src/components/session-tile/status-dot.tsx:13-20,41-48`); attention tiers = 3 (§12.2); status tone hues = 4 (§5.3); plus `MemberStatusDot`'s 4 (deep-1 §7). `error` maps to no mark state and no tier, though it has three dedicated affordances today (ErrorBadge, `StatusBorder` static orange, "Error" label), and `starting` (the spinner state, deliberately fused with `active` per the status-dot comment) also vanishes. `thinking` is a hook turn-state that exists in none of the other vocabularies. **Fix:** B0 ships one mapping table — status × tier × mark state × tone — and either promotes failure to a fourth tier or defines an explicit `failed` mark state; `§16.4`'s "document vocabularies in BRAND.md" is the right home but is scheduled in B5, four fases after the vocabularies ship.

**5. [major] "The Attention card and the roster dot speak the same three tiers" (§12.2) contradicts Part I.** Part I §4.4 defines the Attention card as the *escalation for states chat cannot model* (unknown dialogs, undelivered sends, stale pointer) — while permission prompts and plan approvals, the canonical "needs attention" causes per deep-3 §8, render as **P5 choice cards in chat** and explicitly are *not* Attention cards (§2.7 table rows "Permission prompt"/"Plan approval" → chat). So §12.3's rollup promise, "each face one tap from its Attention card", targets a card that will not exist in the most common case. **Fix:** define the rollup's target as "the session's chat scrolled to the pending P5, falling back to the Attention card", and define the needs-attention tier from the hook/transcript anchors directly, not from Attention-card presence.

**6. [major] The unread tier has neither a data source nor a scope, and its sync mechanism will clobber itself.** §12.2 promises "dot + optional count for discrete items", but the only roster payload is `chat_tail` = "last user one-liner + last assistant/receipt line + ts" (§2.5) — no entry count exists on the wire, so a count is uncomputable. Worse, Track A's guard restricts the transcript stream to `provider==='claude' && host_id==null && !team` (line 20), so Codex, kimi, remote-host and team sessions get *no* unread tier at all while the roster presents a uniform three-tier model. And §13.4 syncs seen-cursors "via `/api/prefs` + SSE" — `server/src/prefs.rs:61-65` is an allowlist of exactly `overview_layout|quick_keys` storing a single opaque ≤50 KB blob with no per-key merge, so phone and desktop will overwrite each other's read state on every session open. **Fix:** add `entry_count`/`last_entry_ts` to the `chat_tail` delta; specify the non-Claude fallback (unread ← `last_activity`/`preview_lines` change); give seen-cursors a dedicated per-session endpoint rather than the prefs blob.

**7. [major] The "one row component ends the tile/row split" claim (§12.1) is asserted, never specified — the split survives under a new label.** deep-1 roughness 5 enumerates the divergence precisely (tile: tokens/branch, ErrorBadge, HostBadge, ⌘1-9 jump chip, StatusBorder pulse, hover live peek, type-on-hover, inline archive; row: status *word* + relative timestamp and nothing else). §12.1's row is `mark · name · time · preview`, and §12.5 keeps tile-only facts (context% "at high density", conversation tail, hover peek). No density→fact ladder exists anywhere in Part II; §19.3 only asks about `chat_tail` line counts. Separately, applying the same live row to "palette rows" means ⌘K entries tick their timestamps and mutate their status line under the keyboard cursor — Grok's picker rows (deep-2 §7) are static. **Fix:** add the fact-ladder table (tile tiers 1–4 · list row · focus strip · picker · palette) to B2's scope and exclude live-mutating fields from palette rows.

**8. [major] deep-1's roughness #4 is the only one the plan leaves unaddressed — and Part II launders it into a strength.** The Part II preamble (line 241) and §12 both assert supermux's grouping/sorting "exceeds Grok", but deep-1 §2 documents it as "the largest complexity-to-value inversion in the product": 3 global sort modes vs 6 per-group modes reachable *only* inside custom mode, per-group sorts persisted to **localStorage** while the global mode is server-persisted — which directly contradicts §13.4's "cross-device continuity … make it *felt*" — no group-by project/dir/provider/host/status, and 2,240 lines of DnD. §14 then adds sort/density/view to the palette, making a **fourth** surface for the same prefs (header chips / mobile Display sheet / Settings→Appearance / palette) with no cross-reference. **Fix:** add a B2 item that (a) moves per-group sort to the server pref, (b) adds group-by presets so grouping isn't only manual drag, (c) names one canonical prefs surface with the others as thin mirrors; and stop citing the current sort IA as an adopted advantage.

**9. [major] Self-naming (§10) destroys the stated justification for skipping the job-title field.** §10 skips Grok's title because "`task_summary` already fills that slot, live" — but `sessionTitle()` (`web/src/lib/api/sessions.ts:182-189`) returns `display_name` whenever it differs from the slug, so an agent-proposed `display_name` after the first prompt permanently shadows the live auto-title on every tile and the focus header. Ship self-naming and the live slot is gone for every session — the decision to skip the title field (deep-3 row 3, correct on its own evidence) becomes unsupported. **Fix:** make identity two-line where the preview isn't already live (display_name 14/20 + `task_summary` secondary), or restrict self-naming to sessions whose `display_name` is still the slug; decide in B0, not implicitly in B2.

**10. [major] The scheduler fold is specified as "pure move — no behavior change" (B1) but is a redesign, and it silently breaks onboarding.** `routes/scheduler.tsx:83` is `max-w-5xl` with a 5-column `[1fr_10rem_7rem_7rem_3rem]` desktop grid (`:154,:196`), a 923-line `ScheduleForm` and `FireLog`; `routes/settings.tsx:812` is `max-w-2xl` with its own control kit (`settings/primitives.tsx`) and scroll container, and §12.7 explicitly preserves Settings' grammar as the sanctioned variant — the table cannot move "wholesale" into 42rem without either widening Settings (breaking §12.7) or redesigning the list (breaking "no behavior change"). Separately, deleting the Scheduler nav item removes `tour: 'scheduler'` (`layout.tsx:56`), which is onboarding tour **step 3** (`tour-overlay.tsx:35`, copy "Schedule the routine", `brand/copy.ts:202+`); it degrades to a centred card pointing at a page that no longer exists. Part II never mentions onboarding at all, despite deep-2 §5 documenting seven concrete onboarding mechanics and §12.6 rewriting the create flow the tour's step 4 anchors to. **Fix:** spec Settings→Schedules as a settings-width list + `ResponsiveSheet` detail (not a move), and re-anchor/rewrite the tour in the same PR.

**Genuinely sound (one line):** §11's shell language (substrate/tint/z-ladder/side-pane grid/overlay/`.sm-swap`/fixed slots/fade masks/facepile/three speeds) is a faithful, near-complete adoption of deep-2's ranked devices; §13.2's delegation UX and §13.1's zero-layout-cost entity chips are the correct top-3 borrow from deep-3; §15 (duplicate, hide-vs-pause contract, delete honesty + undo, per-session notifications, graded recovery) and §16 (modal/empty-state/teams consolidation) map cleanly onto deep-3 rows 23–26 and deep-1 roughnesses 6/8/10.
